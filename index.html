Fair questions

How accurate are the prices?

As accurate as the markets behind them, and it tells you how sure it is. A card has no single true price, so CardCapture takes a weighted median of market figures, where completed sales and recent figures count most, and shows the range when markets disagree. Asking prices on eBay and Amazon are shown for context but never count. For a slab, it won’t invent a graded price, it links to what graded copies actually sold for.

Can it tell if a card is fake?

Not with certainty. A photo can’t prove a card is genuine, and CardCapture never claims it can. It checks the print the way an expert would, dot pattern, ink, type, how the foil moves when you tilt it, and whether the printed details match that printing. You get a verdict in words, a probability when enough checks ran, or “Inconclusive” when they didn’t. A reason to look closer, not a certificate.

Is the grade a real PSA grade?

No. It’s CardCapture’s own estimate on a PSA-style and a BGS-style scale, from photos of the front and the back. Only a grading company can give a card a grade. Use it to decide which cards are worth sending in. If the photos can’t support an estimate, you’re told what to change and nothing is charged.

Does it work offline?

For scanning, yes. For fresh prices, no. Identifying, checking and measuring all happen on your phone; no photo is sent anywhere to be recognized. English Pokémon cards are recognized with no connection at all, because that catalog is built in. Other games download their catalog once, then work offline too. Offline, a card you’ve priced before shows its last price and how old it is.

After that, buy one for $0.99 or five for $2.99, or get CardCapture Pro at $4.99 a month, $29.99 a year or $79.99 once. Pro adds unlimited gradings, whole binder pages, price history, every measurement and CSV export. Both subscriptions start with 14 days free. Prices are in US dollars; the App Store shows yours.

Do you sell my data?

No, there’s nothing to sell. CardCapture has no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Its one server, a price relay, passes lookups on and keeps no record of who asked. To keep count of the three free gradings it uses Apple’s DeviceCheck, which says nothing about who you are. Your collection lives on your phone, and in your own private iCloud if you choose; nobody else can read it, us included. Price lookups name a card, never you. On the App Store, the privacy label says “Data Not Collected”.

How do I scan a whole binder?

One page at a time, with Pro. Switch the scanner to Page, hold your phone square over the page, check the number of cards in view, and take the photo. The cards are identified, priced and listed in reading order, with any it isn’t sure of at the top. Then add them all. Single cards and stacks stay free. For a print check, scan the card on its own.
