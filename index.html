How it works

Scan

You point. It shoots.

Hold a card in view. Once it’s sharp and still, CardCapture takes the photo and names the exact printing, down to set and number.

In the app: the name, set and number, with a ring showing how sure it is. Two printings look alike? It says “More than one card fits” and lets you pick.

Price

A price, and where it came from.

A card has no single true price, so CardCapture doesn’t pretend it does. It combines market prices into one estimate and shows the range when they disagree.

In the app: the price, how sure it is (sometimes that’s “Rough estimate”), and which markets it came from — and how long ago.

Check

It checks the print — and says when it can’t.

Counterfeits are printed differently. CardCapture looks at the dot pattern, the ink, the type and how the foil moves when you tilt the card. It also measures centering, corners, edges and surface.

In the app: a verdict in words, from “Consistent with genuine” to “Strong signs of a counterfeit”, how many checks ran, and the condition with the one thing holding it back. Not enough to go on? “Inconclusive” — and what to change.

Free or Pro, the answer is the same. Pro adds the working: every quote behind a price, what each check measured, and each card’s price history.

Fair questions

How accurate are the prices?

As accurate as the markets behind them, and it tells you how sure it is. A card has no single true price, so CardCapture takes a weighted median of market figures, where completed sales and recent figures count most, and shows the range when markets disagree. Asking prices on eBay and Amazon are shown for context but never count. For a slab, it won’t invent a graded price — it links to what graded copies actually sold for.

Can it tell if a card is fake?

Not with certainty. A photo can’t prove a card is genuine, and CardCapture never claims it can. It checks the print the way an expert would — dot pattern, ink, type, how the foil moves when you tilt it, and whether the printed details match that printing. You get a verdict in words, a probability when enough checks ran, or “Inconclusive” when they didn’t. A reason to look closer, not a certificate.

Does it work offline?

For scanning, yes. For fresh prices, no. Identifying, checking and measuring all happen on your phone; no photo is sent anywhere to be recognized. English Pokémon cards are recognized with no connection at all, because that catalog is built in. Other games download their catalog once, then work offline too. Offline, a card you’ve priced before shows its last price and how old it is.

Do you sell my data?

No — there’s nothing to sell. CardCapture has no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Its one server, a price relay, passes price lookups on and keeps no record of who asked. Your collection lives on your phone, and in your own private iCloud if you choose; nobody else can read it, us included. Price lookups name a card, never you. On the App Store, the privacy label says “Data Not Collected”.

How do I scan a whole binder?

One page at a time. Switch the scanner to Page, hold your phone square over the page, check the number of cards in view, and take the photo. The cards are identified, priced and listed in reading order, with any it isn’t sure of at the top — then add them all. Each identified card uses one scan; Magic and Lorcana never do. For a print check, scan the card on its own.
