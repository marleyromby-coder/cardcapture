Privacy Policy · Last updated 18 November 2026

What CardCapture knows about you.

Very little, and you decide the rest. The app itself keeps no record of you: no analytics, no tracking, no advertising identifier, and no third-party SDK that phones home. Prices come through a small relay that names a card, never you, and writes no log. From version 1.4 there is a community you can join if you want to. If you do, the community's server holds the profile you make and what you post there, and nothing else about you. This page explains exactly what the app stores, what it sends, and where it goes.

The short version
  • Nothing about you is kept by us unless you join the community. Price lookups name a card, never you, and the relay writes no log.
  • The community is optional. From version 1.4, joining makes a profile on CardCapture's own server: a username, a name and a picture you choose, what you post, and how your collection has moved in percent. You can read everything it holds and delete all of it, in the app.
  • Scanning happens on your device — the camera, the text recognition, the artwork matching, the print and condition analysis. No photo is uploaded for recognition.
  • Signing in is offered, not required. If you do, the account is stored on your device — no CardCapture server holds it. Every feature works either way, and a subscription follows your Apple Account rather than any account of ours.
  • With an account, your collection is backed up to your own private iCloud, which we cannot read. Without one, it stays on your device unless you turn iCloud on.
  • Price lookups name a card, never you.

What stays on your device

Your collection and the photos kept with your cards, your scan history, your recorded price history, your saved price alerts and your purchase entitlements are all stored in the app's own container on your device. Scanning a card, a stack or a binder page is free and is not counted.

Two things also go into the iOS keychain: how many of the free condition gradings have been used, so deleting and reinstalling the app does not silently hand out new ones, and any marketplace API keys you choose to enter. Keychain items are readable only by this app on this device.

The free gradings and Apple's DeviceCheck

From version 1.2, the count of free gradings used is also agreed with CardCapture's price relay, so that erasing the phone does not reset it either. The app asks iOS for a DeviceCheck token — a one-time, opaque value Apple issues for the device, which says nothing about you, your Apple Account or your cards — and sends it to the relay with the count. The relay passes it to Apple, which keeps two bits per device for CardCapture: that is the whole record, and it holds only the count. The relay stores and logs nothing, and the count is never tied to an account, a name or an address. Apple's own description of this service is in its developer documentation for DeviceCheck.

Signing in — optional, and nothing reaches us

CardCapture offers to sign you in on first launch, and lets you carry on without an account if you would rather. There are two ways to sign in, and neither of them creates an account on a server, because CardCapture has no account server. What is stored is kept in the iOS keychain on your device, and you can sign in or out later from Settings › Account.

Sign in with Apple

Apple gives the app a stable identifier for you, and — on the first authorisation only — your name if you allow it. Both are kept in the keychain and are used to show who is signed in. CardCapture does not request your email address. If you revoke access in Settings → your name → Sign in with Apple, the app notices and signs you out locally.

Email and password

This one creates a profile on this iPhone only. The address you type and a cryptographic hash of your password (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 210,000 iterations, with a random salt) go into the keychain. The password itself is never stored, and neither the address nor the password is sent anywhere — CardCapture has no account server to send them to, and for the same reason there is no password reset and no way to recover the account if you forget it. The app says so on the screen where you choose this option.

Signing out keeps your collection and, for the email option, keeps the stored credential so you can sign back in.

This is the app's own sign-in. The community (below) has a separate sign-in with Apple, and that one does reach a server.

Deleting your account

From version 1.1, Settings › Account › Delete account deletes the account and everything kept under it: its cards and the photos kept with them, scans, scan photos, condition records and backups on this iPhone, its copy in your private iCloud, and, for the email option, the stored credential. It cannot be undone. A subscription belongs to your Apple Account and is cancelled in the Settings app; for Sign in with Apple, you can also stop CardCapture using your Apple Account in Settings → your name → Sign in with Apple. Without an account, deleting the app deletes everything it kept on the device.

iCloud backup — with an account, or when you turn it on

When you sign in, your collection is written to your own private CloudKit database, under your Apple Account, in Apple's iCloud, so your cards come back when you sign in again — after reinstalling, or on another device signed into the same iCloud. Without an account this happens only if you turn it on. That is your storage, not ours. The developer of CardCapture has no access to it, and neither does any other CardCapture user. You can turn it off in Settings › Account; turning it off stops further writes, and deleting the app or signing out of iCloud removes the app's access.

The app also keeps a copy of your collection on your device for each of the last seven days, and before anything removes more than half of it, so a mistake can be undone from Settings › Account › Backups. These copies never leave the device.

What leaves your device, and to whom

Every request below identifies a card. None carries an account, a device identifier, or anything about you. These providers see an ordinary web request and its IP address, and their own logging applies.

WhenWhere it goes
You download a card database Scryfall (Magic), YGOPRODeck (Yu-Gi-Oh), OnePieceJSON on GitHub (One Piece), digimoncard.io (Digimon), gcg-api on GitHub (Gundam), the ApiTCG data repositories on GitHub (Dragon Ball, Union Arena, and One Piece or Digimon when their first source cannot be reached), LorcanaJSON (Lorcana), the-fab-cube on GitHub (Flesh and Blood), and TCGdex (Japanese Pokémon). Pokémon in English ships inside the app and downloads nothing. A downloaded database is fetched again about once a week, on Wi-Fi, so new sets arrive.
You price a card The Pokémon TCG API, TCGdex (Japanese Pokémon, and from version 1.1 Cardmarket prices for English Pokémon), Scryfall or YGOPRODeck, depending on the game.

From version 1.1, graded prices, and prices for One Piece, Lorcana, Digimon, Dragon Ball, Union Arena and Flesh and Blood, come through CardCapture's price relay: a Cloudflare Worker that asks JustTCG with CardCapture's own key. It is sent the card's game and its name or printed code, and for a graded price the grader and the grade — nothing else. Like any server on the internet it sees the IP address a request comes from; it uses it for at most a minute, to stop any one address flooding it, and never stores or logs it. Answers are kept by card for up to a week, so the next lookup of the same card is answered without asking JustTCG again; they say nothing about who asked. Cloudflare runs the relay and handles the connection as it does for any website.

If you have supplied your own eBay or JustTCG keys, that provider too — using your keys, for that one lookup, straight from your device.
You check a slab's certificate, or its sold prices The grader's own site (PSA, CGC, Beckett, SGC or TAG) opens with the certificate number read from the label, or eBay's sold listings or PriceCharting's price guide open with the card and its grade. Nothing about you is sent.
You tap Find it for sale The store you picked — eBay or Cardmarket — opens in its own app or your browser, with the card's name, set and number in the address. Nothing about you is sent, and nothing is sent at all until you tap. What you do on the store's site is between you and the store, under its own privacy policy.
You open Market From version 1.2, CardCapture's price relay asks JustTCG for the most valuable cards of a fixed list of popular sets and their last 30 days of sales. The request names a set, never you or your cards, and is the same for everyone who opens Market; the answer is kept on your device for six hours, and the relay keeps it by set and writes no log, as above.
You open Shop From version 1.2, CardCapture's price relay asks JustTCG for the newest sets of the game you pick and what their sealed products and most traded cards sell for, and asks eBay for the cheapest Buy It Now listings of those products and cards that ship to the country set on your phone. The requests name products, cards and a country, never you; the relay keeps the answers for up to six hours and writes no log, as above. Listing photos load from eBay's image servers, and tapping one opens it on eBay, under eBay's own privacy policy.
You open a card, a scan or a wish, and it shows what is on eBay now From version 1.2, CardCapture's price relay asks eBay for that card's Buy It Now listings. It sends eBay the card's name and printed number (and its grade, for a slab), the eBay site for your phone's region, and the country set on your phone, so postage is worked out to it and only listings that ship there come back. eBay is asked by the relay, not by your phone, and is told nothing about you; the relay keeps the answer by card for up to six hours and writes no log, as above. The listings' photos load from eBay's image servers, which, like any image on the web, see the IP address that asks for them. Tapping a listing opens it on eBay, in its app or your browser, under eBay's own privacy policy.
A price alert checks in The same price providers, and the price relay for the cards it prices, from your device, in the background. No server is told which cards you watch: the watching happens on your phone, and a lookup for a watched card looks like any other.
You buy a subscription or gradings Apple, through StoreKit. CardCapture is told only whether a purchase is active. It never sees your payment details.

The launch waitlist (this website only)

Before the app is on the App Store, the website offers to send you one message when it is. The app itself has no waitlist and sends nothing to it.

What is kept: the email address you type, the language of the page you typed it on (English or Danish), and the day you joined. Not your IP address, your browser, or anything else about you. Why: to send one email on the day CardCapture is released — nothing else, and not to anyone else. Where: in a store run by Cloudflare, the company that also runs CardCapture's price relay, under CardCapture's own account. Like any server on the internet it sees the address your request comes from; it uses it for at most a minute, to stop one address from flooding the list, and does not store or log it. The page asks for nothing else, sets no cookies and has no analytics.

How long: until that one message has been sent, and then the list is deleted. Your choices: ask on the support page and your address is deleted; you will get no message. The legal basis is your consent, given by submitting the form, which you can withdraw at any time the same way.

The community — optional, and the one place we keep something

From version 1.4 the app has a community: collectors following each other and posting about their cards. You do not have to join it, and the rest of the app works the same if you do not. Until you join, opening the tab sends nothing about you.

Joining. You are asked the month and year you were born. That answer stays on your phone; the community is for people aged 13 and over, and the server is told only that the age is met. You then sign in with Apple. Apple tells our server that it is you; we ask for neither your name nor your email. The server keeps a scrambled form of Apple's identifier for you, which lets it recognise you next time and cannot be turned back into the identifier.

What the server holds once you have a profile

  • the username and name you choose, and your picture: a letter on a colour, one of your own cards, or a photo you choose to upload;
  • your posts and comments, what you like, whom you follow and whom you have blocked;
  • how many cards you hold and how your collection has changed in percent over a week, a month, three months and a year, with the shape of its line;
  • for each binder you choose to show: its name and its cards, each with its change in percent over the past month. No price is sent for any card;
  • what your collection is worth, only while you have switched "Show what my collection is worth" on. It is off until you turn it on, it is offered only to members aged 18 and over, and switching it off removes the figure from the server at once;
  • if you have Pro: that you do, so a check can show beside your name. The app names one purchase to the server, which asks Apple about it and keeps a scrambled form of the purchase and the date the check runs to;
  • reports you make, until they have been answered, and what was decided.

A profile photo, if you upload one, is cut to a small square on your phone, and the place it was taken and other details stored in the picture are removed before it is sent. Before anyone else sees it, it is checked automatically by an AI model run for us by Cloudflare, which does not keep it or learn from it; a photo the check is unsure about is looked at by the developer. Until it has been approved, others see your letter. A private profile's photo is shown only to the people you have accepted. You can remove it at any time, and it is deleted with your profile.

Following. When you join, your profile follows CardCapture's own account, @cardcapture, for news about the app and new sets. You can unfollow it at any time. Only that account can see the list of who follows it.

Who sees it. A profile is private until you open it. Private: only people you accept see your collection figures and your posts; anyone in the community can find your username, name and picture and ask to follow you. Public: everyone in the community can see them. Nothing in the community is on the web, and none of it is shown to anyone outside the app.

What we do with it. We run the community with it, and nothing else. It is not used for advertising, not used to track you, not sold and not shared. Reports are read by the developer, who can remove a post, a comment or a profile that breaks the community terms.

Where it is. On a server and database run for CardCapture by Cloudflare, with the database kept in the European Union. Like any server it sees the address a request comes from; it uses it for a minute, to stop one address from flooding it, and does not store or log it. The server writes no log of what you do.

How long. Until you delete it. A post or comment that was removed for breaking the terms is kept out of sight for 30 days, in case the decision is challenged, and then deleted. A profile not opened for 24 months is deleted. A profile removed for breaking the terms is deleted after 30 days, and a scrambled marker is kept for 12 months so the same Apple Account cannot simply join again.

Your choices. In the app, on your profile: change or hide what it shows, get a copy of everything the server holds about you, sign out, or delete the profile. Deleting removes the profile, your posts, comments, likes and follows at once; Apple asks you to confirm that it is you. You can also write to support@cardcaptureapp.site.

The legal basis is the agreement you enter by joining: we cannot show a profile without keeping one. You can end it at any time by deleting the profile.

Camera and photos

The camera is used to scan cards and, if you ask for it, to capture the tilt and edge photographs the verification checks need. All of that processing happens on your device. CardCapture does not request access to your photo library. From version 1.4 the camera can also take a community profile photo, or you can pick one in the system's photo picker, which hands the app only the picture you choose; what happens to it is described under The community.

From version 1.3, when you scan a card that has no catalog picture and add it to your collection, the app keeps your own photo of that card, cropped to the card, and shows it as the card's picture, marked Your photo. The photo is kept in the app's storage on this device only. It is not sent to us or to anyone else, it is not part of the iCloud copy of your collection, and it is not used in widgets or in the year review you can share. Like everything the app stores, it is included in your own iPhone backup if you keep one. You can remove the photo on the card's page, and you can turn this off in Settings. The photo is also deleted when you remove the card, delete your account or delete the app. A condition record (card passport) likewise keeps the photo it was made from, on this device.

Separately, if you turn on Keep a photo of each scan in Settings › Scan log, a photo of every scan, whether or not you add the card, is kept on your device — the newest 300 — and included when you export the scan log yourself. That is off unless you turn it on. None of these photos is ever sent anywhere by the app.

Notifications

Price alerts are local notifications, created on your device by your device. CardCapture does not use push notifications and has no ability to send you one.

Widgets

The CardCapture widgets on your Home Screen, Lock Screen and in Control Center either open the scanner or show your collection's value, card count and when it was last valued. The app writes those few figures to a container on your device that only CardCapture and its own widgets can read; nothing is sent anywhere for them.

Children

The app itself is for collectors of any age and keeps nothing about anyone. The community is for people aged 13 and over: the app asks the month and year of birth before anyone can join, keeps the answer on the phone, and does not let anyone younger make a profile. If you believe someone under 13 has a profile, report it in the app or write to support@cardcaptureapp.site and it will be removed.

What Apple's privacy label says

From version 1.4, CardCapture's App Store page lists, under Data Linked to You, what the community holds for those who join it: a user ID (the username), a name (the one you choose), user content (your posts and what your profile shows), photos (a profile photo, if you upload one), and purchase history (that you have Pro, for the check beside your name). All five are used only to make the community work, and none is used to track you. Everything else the app handles stays on your device or names a card rather than you, which by Apple's definition is not collected. The app ships a PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy manifest that says the same. Before version 1.4 the page said Data Not Collected, which was true then: there was no community. This page is the readable version of that declaration.

Changes
