What CardCapture knows about you.

Almost nothing, and none of it reaches us. There is no CardCapture server. No analytics, no tracking, no advertising identifier, and no third-party SDK that phones home. This page explains exactly what the app stores, what it sends, and where it goes.

The short version
  • Nothing is sent to us, because there is nowhere to send it. CardCapture has no backend.
  • Scanning happens on your device — the camera, the text recognition, the artwork matching, the print and condition analysis. No photo is uploaded for recognition.
  • Signing in is optional and unlocks nothing. Every feature works signed out.
  • iCloud sync is off until you turn it on, and writes to your own private iCloud storage, which we cannot read.
  • Price lookups name a card, never you.

What stays on your device

Your collection, your scan history, your recorded price history, your saved price alerts, your free-scan count and your purchase entitlements are all stored in the app's own container on your device.

Two things also go into the iOS keychain: the free-scan count, so deleting and reinstalling the app does not silently reset your remaining free scans, and any marketplace API keys you choose to enter. Keychain items are readable only by this app on this device.

Sign in with Apple — optional

If you sign in, Apple gives the app a stable identifier for you, and — on the first authorisation only — your name if you allow it. Both are kept in the keychain on your device and are used to show who is signed in. Neither is transmitted anywhere: there is no server to receive them.

CardCapture does not request your email address. If you revoke access in Settings → your name → Sign in with Apple, the app notices and signs you out locally. Signing out deletes the stored identifier.

iCloud sync — optional, and off by default

When you enable sync, your collection is written to your own private CloudKit database, under your Apple Account, in Apple's iCloud. That is your storage, not ours. The developer of CardCapture has no access to it, and neither does any other CardCapture user. Turning sync off stops further writes; deleting the app or signing out of iCloud removes the app's access.

What leaves your device, and to whom

Every request below identifies a card. None carries an account, a device identifier, or anything about you. These providers see an ordinary web request and its IP address, and their own logging applies.

WhenWhere it goes
You download a card database Scryfall (Magic), YGOPRODeck (Yu-Gi-Oh), the ApiTCG data repositories on GitHub (One Piece, Digimon, Dragon Ball, Union Arena), LorcanaJSON (Lorcana), and the-fab-cube on GitHub (Flesh and Blood). Pokémon ships inside the app and downloads nothing.
You price a card The Pokémon TCG API, Scryfall or YGOPRODeck, depending on the game. If you have supplied your own eBay, Amazon or JustTCG keys, that provider too — using your keys, for that one lookup.
A price alert checks in The same price providers, from your device, in the background. No server is told which cards you watch, because the watching happens on your phone.
You buy a subscription or a scan pack Apple, through StoreKit. CardCapture is told only whether a purchase is active. It never sees your payment details.

Camera and photos

The camera is used to scan cards and, if you ask for it, to capture the tilt and edge photographs the verification checks need. All of that processing happens on your device. CardCapture does not request access to your photo library.

Notifications

Price alerts are local notifications, created on your device by your device. CardCapture does not use push notifications and has no ability to send you one.

Children

CardCapture is for collectors of any age and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included.

What Apple's privacy label says

The app ships a PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy manifest declaring no tracking, and two data types — User ID and Name — both solely from optional Sign in with Apple, both linked to you by definition, and both used only to make the app work. Nothing else is declared because nothing else is collected. This page is the readable version of that declaration.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Material changes will be described in the App Store release notes for the version that introduces them.

Contact

Questions about this policy, or a request about your data, can be sent through the support link on the CardCapture App Store listing, which reaches the developer directly.
