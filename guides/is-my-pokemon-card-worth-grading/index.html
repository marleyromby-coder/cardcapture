Is my Pokémon card worth grading?

Grading is worth it when the gap between what your card sells for raw and what it would sell for graded is bigger than what grading costs you, after allowing for the chance it comes back lower than you hoped. Here is how to work that out for one card.

Updated 11 November 2026 · Prices from the CardCapture app

The short version

Grade a card when what it would sell for graded, on average, minus what grading costs is clearly more than what it sells for raw. “On average” is the part people skip: not every card comes back a 10.

The gap between raw and PSA 10

A raw card is an ungraded one. A PSA 10 is a copy a grading company has examined and judged gem mint, sealed in a slab with its grade on the label. Buyers pay more for a 10 because it is scarce and because the grade is settled. That extra is the raw-to-10 gap, and it is different for every card.

  • The gap tends to be widest on cards that are popular and hard to find perfect: chase cards, cards whose print often comes off-centre, and older cards that have been handled for years.
  • The gap tends to be small on cheap cards and on cards that often come out of the pack perfect. When the grading fee is close to the card’s raw price, grading rarely pays.

A 10 is not the only result

This is where most of the money is lost. For many modern cards a PSA 9 sells for not much more than a raw copy, sometimes less once fees are counted. So the real question is not “what is a 10 worth?” but “how likely is a 10, and what happens if it isn’t?”

Before you guess, look at the card the way a grader would, under a bright light, front and back:

  • Centering: are the borders even, left to right and top to bottom? Check the back too.
  • Corners: any white on the back corners is a strong sign it will not get a 10.
  • Edges: chips or whitening along the back edges.
  • Surface: scratches, print lines, dents or marks, seen when you tilt the card in the light.

Costs people forget

  • The fee depends on the card’s declared value, and faster service costs more. Check the grading company’s current price list before you decide.
  • Shipping both ways and insurance, per card if you are only sending one or two.
  • Time. The card is gone for weeks, sometimes months, and the market can move while it is away.

When grading usually makes sense

  • A valuable card with clean corners, edges and surface, and even centering front and back.
  • A card where even a 9 sells for well over the raw price plus the costs.
  • A card you plan to keep for years and want protected, with its condition settled.

When it usually does not

  • The card is worth less than the fee raw, and a 10 does not change that much.
  • You can see whitening, a scratch or clearly uneven borders.
  • The card is from a set that came out weeks ago and has few graded sales, like Delta Reign. Wait until there are some.

Questions

How much more is a PSA 10 worth than a raw card?

There is no fixed multiple. For some cards a 10 sells for many times the raw price; for others it is barely more. Look it up for the exact card: CardCapture’s price by grade shows each grade as a multiple of the ungraded price.

Is a PSA 9 worth it?

Only if a 9 of that card sells for clearly more than a raw copy plus everything grading cost you. For many modern cards a 9 sells close to raw, so a card that is not a 10 can lose money once fees are paid.

Can an app tell me what grade my card will get?

No. Only a grading company can grade a card. CardCapture measures centering, corners, edges and surface from photos of the front and back and gives its own estimate on a PSA-style and a BGS-style scale. Use it to decide which cards are worth sending in.

Should I grade cards from a brand-new set?

Be patient. In the first weeks there are few graded sales, so nobody knows yet what a 10 of that card is worth, and raw prices are still moving. Keep the card sleeved and protected, and run the numbers again once graded sales appear.
